## 1. Türkiye — Kavala judgment round two lands on 25 August
**Treaty:** European Convention on Human Rights (Arts. 5, 18, 46)
**Country:** Türkiye

The ECtHR Grand Chamber will deliver its ruling in **Kavala v. Türkiye (no. 2)** on **25 August 2026**. The case concerns Osman Kavala's continued detention *after* the Court's 2019 judgment already found his imprisonment unlawful and politically motivated, and the later proceedings that gave him an aggravated life sentence.

**Why it matters to you:** This is the clearest live test of whether a European government can simply ignore a binding Strasbourg judgment. If a state can keep a person locked up after the Court orders release, every other ECHR right — yours included — becomes advisory rather than enforceable.

## 2. Italy — parliamentary inquiry search of Masonic lodge violated the right to a private home
**Treaty:** ECHR Art. 8 (respect for the home / private life)
**Country:** Italy

In **Grande Oriente d'Italia v. Italy** (Grand Chamber, 7 July 2026) the Court found a **violation** of the right to respect for the home. A parliamentary anti-mafia commission searched the association's premises and seized paper and digital documents including lists with the **personal data of more than six thousand people** — without sufficient legal safeguards.

**Why it matters to you:** Investigations run by parliaments, not judges, can sweep up the data of thousands of uninvolved citizens. Strasbourg has now said: no independent safeguards, no search. That standard protects anyone whose name sits in someone else's file.

## 3. Portugal — you can face several proceedings, but only under strict conditions
**Treaty:** ECHR Protocol No. 7, Art. 4 (*ne bis in idem* — no double jeopardy)
**Country:** Portugal

In **Jesus Pinhal v. Portugal** (Grand Chamber, 9 July 2026) the Court found **no violation**. A former bank vice-chairman faced criminal, securities-regulator and central-bank proceedings over overlapping facts. The Court used the case to clarify the criteria for balancing effective punishment of financial crime against the right not to be tried twice.

**Why it matters to you:** Ordinary people are increasingly hit by parallel administrative *and* criminal penalties (tax, traffic, benefits). This judgment sets the boundary line for when that is lawful stacking and when it is punishment twice over.

Source: https://www.echr.coe.int/ · Video explainer: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MvADW6hT64k

## 4. Russia / Ukraine — ten Ukrainian children get a Strasbourg hearing
**Treaty:** ECHR (Arts. 3, 5, 8) alongside CRC obligations
**Countries:** Russia, Ukraine

The Court has scheduled a public hearing for **22 September 2026** in *Ukrainian Helsinki Human Rights Union on behalf of ten Ukrainian children v. Russia*, concerning the deportation and transfer of children.

**Why it matters to you:** Child deportation in wartime is one of the gravest treaty breaches on the books. A public inter-state-style hearing creates an official evidentiary record — the kind of record that later underpins returns, reparations and prosecutions.

Source: https://www.echr.coe.int/e/news-copy-/20132/10743977

## 5. UN — Jordan pressed on judicial independence; HRC61 targets Afghanistan, DPRK, Iran
**Treaties:** ICCPR (Art. 14), CEDAW
**Countries:** Jordan; Afghanistan, DPR Korea, Iran

On **5 August 2026** UN High Commissioner Volker Türk visited Jordan and publicly encouraged **strengthening judicial independence** (ICCPR Art. 14 fair-trial guarantees). In parallel, civil-society submissions ahead of the Human Rights Council's **61st session** flag Taliban restrictions on Afghan women and girls as violations of **CEDAW**, and document gender persecution in Iran and systematic violations in the DPRK.

**Why it matters to you:** Judicial independence is the machinery that makes every other right claimable — without an independent judge, a ratified treaty is a leaflet. And CEDAW is the specific promise Afghanistan's obligations rest on when girls are barred from school.
