## 1. Predictive Policing – Polizei & Justiz in Deutschland

- **System:** Geografische Kriminalitätsvorhersage ("predictive policing"), individual profilerende Algorithmen, KI-gestützte Datenanalyse in Polizei, Justiz und Gefängnissen.
- **Wo eingesetzt:** Deutschland — Polizeien der Länder, Staatsanwaltschaften, Justizvollzug; viele Systeme werden ohne ausreichende Rechtsgrundlage entwickelt, getestet oder betrieben.
- **Welches Recht berührt:** EU AI Act Art. 5 (Verbote: Risikobewertung rein auf Basis von Profiling/Persönlichkeitsmerkmalen), Art. 22 DSGVO (Recht auf menschliches Eingreifen), Diskriminierungsverbote (AGG, Grundrechte).
- **Bürgerliche Anspruchsgrundlagen:** Antrag auf Akteneinsicht, Datenschutzbeschwerde bei der Aufsichtsbehörde, Verfassungsbeschwerde bei Grundrechtsverletzung, zivilrechtliche Schadensersatzforderung.
- **Quelle:** [AlgorithmWatch: Automating Injustice — Predictive Policing in Deutschland (28.03.2025)](https://algorithmwatch.org/en/automating-injustice-predictive-policing-germany/)

### EN
- **Headline:** AI-Powered Predictive Policing in Germany Lacks Adequate Legal Basis
- **What changed:** New AlgorithmWatch report documents rising use of geographic crime prediction, individual profiling and AI data analysis by German police, prosecutors and prisons.
- **Why:** Many contested systems operate without proper legal basis; they can reinforce and amplify discrimination.
- **Effective:** Report published 28 March 2025; operational use has been increasing in recent years.
- **Deadline:** EU AI Act Art. 5 prohibitions are already in force (since 2 February 2025); high-risk obligations under Annex III will apply from 2 December 2027.
- **Who's affected:** People in areas subject to police data analysis, especially minority and low-income communities.
- **How:** Citizens can request file access, file a data protection complaint with the supervisory authority, or — in cases of fundamental rights violations — file a constitutional complaint.
- **Citizen tip:** If you suspect an automated profiling decision affected you, ask which system made the decision, on what data, and under what legal basis. Log everything and escalate via your local Datenschutzbeauftragter.

## 2. KI-Modul in der Steuerveranlagung — NRW Pilotprojekt

- **System:** KI-Modul zur Unterstützung der Steuerveranlagung; erkennt Muster in Steuerdaten und identifiziert risikoarme Fälle mit geringem Prüfbedarf für automatische Bearbeitung.
- **Wo eingesetzt:** Ab Mai 2025 in vier Pilotfinanzämtern in NRW (Brühl, Bielefeld-Außenstadt, Hamm, Lübbecke); Start mit klassischen Arbeitnehmerfällen; geplante Landesweit-Einführung nach Testlauf.
- **Welches Recht berührt:** EU AI Act Annex III (öffentliche Stellen, die berechtigt sind, Leistungen/Gesundheitsdienste zu bewilligen, zu reduzieren, zu entziehen oder wiederherzustellen — also Steuerleistungen), Art. 22 DSGVO (automatisierte Entscheidung mit Rechtsfolge), bestehendes Steuergeheimnis.
- **Bürgerliche Anspruchsgrundlagen:** Widerspruch gegen Steuerbescheid, Auskunftsanfrage (Art. 15 DSGVO), Beschwerde bei Landesdatenschutzbeauftragtem NRW, Klage vor Finanzgericht.
- **Quelle:** [Finanzverwaltung NRW: NRW setzt als erstes Bundesland auf KI in der Steuererklärung (22.04.2025)](https://www.finanzverwaltung.nrw.de/uebersicht-rubrik-aktuelles-und-presse/pressemitteilungen/nordrhein-westfalen-setzt-als-erstes)

### EN
- **Headline:** First German State Deploys AI for Tax Assessments — Citizens Need to Know Their Rights
- **What changed:** NRW's finance administration launched a KI module in four pilot tax offices in May 2025; the module automates processing of low-risk employee tax returns identified by pattern recognition.
- **Why:** Tax assessments produce legal effects (refunds, debts); automated eligibility decisions are "high-risk" under EU AI Act Annex III and may fall under Art. 22 GDPR if they determine outcomes solely by algorithm.
- **Effective:** Pilot running since May 2025; no public date for statewide rollout yet.
- **Deadline:** Transparency obligations under Art. 50 AI Act start 2 August 2026 (grace period to 2 December 2026); full high-risk obligations under Annex III apply from 2 December 2027.
- **Who's affected:** Employees filing standard tax returns in NRW; eventually all NRW taxpayers if rollout expands.
- **How:** Citizens can object to the tax assessment (Widerspruch), request data access under Art. 15 GDPR, complain to the NRW Data Protection Commissioner, or bring a case before the Finanzgericht.
- **Citizen tip:** If your tax assessment was processed unusually fast, ask whether a KI system assisted the decision and under what criteria. You always have the right to a human review and a written explanation.

## 3. SCHUFA·Kredit Scoring → EuGH: automatische Entscheidung nach Art. 22 DSGVO

- **System:** SCHUFA-Kredit scoring — algorithmische Wahrscheinlichkeitswerte zur Kreditwürdigkeitsbeurteilung; hat bestimmende Rolle bei Kreditvergabe.
- **Wo eingesetzt:** Deutschland / EU — Banken, Finanzindustrie, Mietwohnungsmarkt, Telekommunikation, Versicherungen nutzen SCHUFA-Werte.
- **Welches Recht berührt:** Art. 22 DSGVO (Recht, nicht alleiniger Unterjektion automatisierter Entscheidung), EU AI Act Annex III (KI-Systeme zur Bewertung der Kreditwürdigkeit = high-risk, außer bei Betrugserkennung).
- **Bürgerliche Anspruchsgrundlagen:** Auskunft nach Art. 15 DSGVO (welche Daten, wie Scoring zustande kam), Widerspruch gegen automatisierte Entscheidung, Datenschutzbeschwerde, Schadensersatz nach Art. 82 DSGVO.
- **Quelle:** [CJEU: Credit score constitutes automated decision-making under Art. 22 GDPR — SCHUFA Holding case](https://tech-litigation.com/the-first-judgment-on-article-22-gdpr-the-schufa-holding-case/)
- **Zusatzquelle:** [gdpr-info.eu: Art. 22 DSGVO — Automated individual decision-making](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/)

### EN
- **Headline:** CJEU Confirms Credit Scoring Is Automated Decision-Making — Citizens Can Challenge SCHUFA Scores
- **What changed:** The European Court of Justice ruled in the SCHUFA Holding case that generating a credit repayment probability score counts as automated individual decision-making under Art. 22 GDPR — when a third party uses it to determine credit granting.
- **Why:** Credit scoring by SCHUFA and similar agencies now falls under GDPR Art. 22 protections. It is also classifiable as a "high-risk" AI system under EU AI Act Annex III (creditworthiness evaluation).
- **Effective:** CJEU ruling is binding; Art. 22 has applied since GDPR entered into force (2018). AI Act high-risk obligations for creditworthiness scoring apply from 2 December 2027.
- **Deadline:** AI Act high-risk obligations for creditworthiness scoring: 2 December 2027.
- **Who's affected:** Anyone in the EU whose SCHUFA score affects credit, rental housing, mobile contracts, insurance or similar decisions.
- **How:** Request your SCHUFA data under Art. 15 GDPR; exercise your right to contest an automated decision; file a data protection complaint; seek damages under Art. 82 GDPR if you suffered harm.
- **Citizen tip:** Every year you can request a free SCHUFA credit report ("SCHUFA-Auskunft"). Check it for errors and request correction. If a decision was made solely by score, demand human review.

## 4. EU AI Act — Implementierungs-Meilensteine

- **Was:** EU-Künstliche-Intelligenz-Gesetz (AI Act) mit risikobasierter Regulierung; Verbote für unakzeptable Risiken, hohe Anforderungen für High-Risk-Systeme.
- **Status:** Verabschiedet; tritt schrittweise in Kraft. AI Omnibus (Vereinfungsvorschlag) — politische Übereinkunft 7. Mai 2026, in Kraft getreten 27. Juli 2026.
- **Welches Recht:** Bürgerrechte durch AI Act: Transparenzpflichten, Registrierung in EU-Datenbank, menschliche Aufsicht, Verbote (soziales Scoring, subliminale Manipulation, biometrische Kategorisierung sensibler Merkmale, unzustätzige Bildgebung).
- **Quelle:** [EU AI Act — official implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)
- **Zusatzquelle:** [European Commission — AI Act regulatory framework](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

### EN
- **Headline:** EU AI Act Timeline — What Takes Effect When
- **What changed:** The AI Act is being implemented in phases. Prohibitions on unacceptable-risk AI (e.g. social scoring, subliminal manipulation, biometric categorisation of sensitive attributes) are already in force since 2 February 2025. Transparency rules under Art. 50 (labelling AI-generated content, notifying users when they interact with AI) apply from 2 August 2026 with a grace period to 2 December 2026. High-risk AI rules under Annex III (credit scoring, public benefit eligibility, insurance risk assessment, employment screening) apply from 2 December 2027. AI Omnibus to simplify the Act entered into force 27 July 2026.
- **Why:** Citizens need to know when their rights under the AI Act crystallise and when authorities and companies must comply.
- **Effective:** Phased timeline from 2 February 2025 through 2 August 2028.
- **Deadline:** Key dates: 2 Aug 2026 (transparency), 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III high-risk), 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I high-risk embedded in regulated products).
- **Who's affected:** EU citizens subjected to automated decisions in employment, credit, insurance, public services, policing.
- **How:** Citizens can invoke transparency and explanation rights; regulators enforce via market surveillance authorities.
- **Citizen tip:** Keep a personal timeline of AI Act deadlines — when a system you encounter must comply affects your ability to demand accountability. Mark 2 August 2026 (transparency) and 2 December 2027 (high-risk) in your calendar.
