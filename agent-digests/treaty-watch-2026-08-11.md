# 🌍 Treaty-Watch — 2026-08-11

A watchdog digest of what governments **promised** under international human-rights law and what they are **actually being held to account for**. Every item cites an official source. Today's run: 5 findings — 3 from the European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) and 2 from the UN Committee against Torture (CAT).

---

## 1. ECHR — Italy: A parliamentary anti-mafia probe illegally raided a civic association
- **Treaty:** European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), Article 8 — right to respect for private and family life / the home.
- **Country:** Italy 🇮🇹
- **What happened:** On 7 July 2026 the Grand Chamber ruled in *Grande Oriente d'Italia v. Italy* that a search and seizure ordered by Parliament's anti-mafia inquiry commission — which swept up the premises of a Masonic association and seized lists with the **names and personal data of more than 6,000 people** — violated Article 8. The Court found the measure had no *ex ante* safeguard and no *ex post* remedy against abuse.
- **Why it matters to ordinary people:** If a parliamentary body can raid a lawful civic group and hoover up the personal data of thousands of members with no judicial check, any association you belong to is exposed. The ruling protects freedom of association and privacy from unchecked state power.
- **Source:** https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?library=ECHR&id=003-8584502-12210439&filename=Grand%20Chamber%20Judgment%20Grande%20Oriente%20d%27Italia%20v.%20Italy%20-%20Search%20and%20seizures%20ordered%20by%20Parliamentary%20Commission%20of%20Inquiry%20on%20combating%20the%20Mafia.pdf

## 2. ECHR — Switzerland: A pregnant prisoner was denied her partner at the birth of her child
- **Treaty:** ECHR, Article 8 — right to respect for private and family life.
- **Country:** Switzerland 🇨🇭
- **What happened:** In *A.G. v. Switzerland* (judgment of 23 July 2026), the Court found a violation of Article 8 after authorities refused a Spanish national in pre-trial detention permission for her partner — the presumed father of her unborn child — to visit her during pregnancy and to be present at the birth.
- **Why it matters to ordinary people:** The bond between a parent and newborn is a human right, not a privilege the state can switch off because someone is detained. The ruling sets a floor for how prisons must treat pregnant prisoners and new mothers.
- **Source:** https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?library=ECHR&id=003-8597152-12233834&filename=Judgments%20of%2023.07.2026.pdf

## 3. ECHR — Ukraine: Even in wartime, indefinite pre-trial detention must be justified
- **Treaty:** ECHR, Article 5 § 3 — right to liberty and security (length of pre-trial detention).
- **Country:** Ukraine 🇺🇦
- **What happened:** In *Derevyanko and Tarasova v. Ukraine* (23 July 2026), the Court found a violation of Article 5 § 3 over unreasonably long, unjustified pre-trial detention under wartime national-security legislation. Two applicants were held for economic activity accused of "assisting the aggressor State." (A related case, *Kolesnyk and Smelnytskyy*, found no violation.)
- **Why it matters to ordinary people:** War does not suspend the right not to be locked up without a proper, reviewed justification. This protects ordinary citizens caught in security investigations from open-ended detention.
- **Source:** https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?library=ECHR&id=003-8597152-12233834&filename=Judgments%20of%2023.07.2026.pdf

## 4. UN — Pakistan: Forced return of Afghan refugees risks torture; concern over political prisoners' medical care
- **Treaty:** UN Convention against Torture (CAT).
- **Country:** Pakistan 🇵🇰
- **What happened:** In its concluding observations (adopted 1 May 2026, 84th session), the CAT expressed *deep concern* over Pakistan's deportation of large numbers of Afghan nationals — including children — under the "Illegal Foreigners' Repatriation Plan" without individual assessment of torture risk (refoulement). It also urged review of persons detained on political grounds — naming former PM Imran Khan — and insisted they get adequate medical care while detained.
- **Why it matters to ordinary people:** The non-refoulement principle means a state must not send anyone back to face torture. Mass expulsions without case-by-case checks put vulnerable refugees, including kids, in danger — and the same logic protects any critic the state wants to silence.
- **Source:** https://www.ungeneva.org/en/news-media/meeting-summary/2026/05/le-comite-contre-la-torture-acheve-les-travaux-de-sa-84e-session

## 5. UN — Italy: Pushbacks, Libya coastguard cooperation and migrant detention in Albania draw fire
- **Treaty:** UN Convention against Torture (CAT).
- **Country:** Italy 🇮🇹
- **What happened:** Also at the CAT's 84th session (1 May 2026), the Committee raised concerns over allegations of pushbacks at Italy's borders, continued cooperation with the Libyan coastguard, and the use of migration-detention facilities in Albania. It recommended upholding non-refoulement, reviewing the Libya MoU, and capping solitary confinement at 15 consecutive days as a last resort.
- **Why it matters to ordinary people:** How a country treats people at its border — including asylum seekers — is a treaty obligation, not a discretionary policy. Solitary-confinement limits also protect anyone held in detention, migrant or citizen.
- **Source:** https://www.ungeneva.org/en/news-media/meeting-summary/2026/05/le-comite-contre-la-torture-acheve-les-travaux-de-sa-84e-session
