## 1. Animal-husbandry label (Tierhaltungskennzeichnung) delayed — again

**What happened:** On 15 January 2026 the Bundestag passed a second amendment to the
Tierhaltungskennzeichnungsgesetz (Drs. 21/3292), pushing back the *obligation* to use the
state animal-husbandry label beyond the previously agreed deadline of 1 March 2026.
An earlier amendment (Drs. 21/327, passed 26 June 2025) had already moved the transition
deadline by seven months, from 1 August 2025 to 1 March 2026.

**Who's behind it:** The CDU/CSU–SPD coalition factions introduced the bill. The
Agrarministerkonferenz of the Länder (28 March 2025) formally asked the federal government
to postpone the deadlines. Food businesses argued enforcement structures in the Länder were
created too late.

**Industry position:** A labelling obligation before the planned fundamental reform would
"lead to uncertainty in the sector"; businesses need time and legal certainty.

**Counter-evidence / opposition:** Bündnis 90/Die Grünen tabled a motion (Drs. 21/578) to
keep the original 1 August 2025 date for fresh pork — rejected by CDU/CSU, AfD and SPD.
A further Green motion (Drs. 21/3633) demanding a commitment to restructuring livestock
farming was also rejected. Net effect: the law has been in force since 24 August 2023 and
consumers still cannot rely on the label at the shelf.

**Why it matters to citizens:** The label is the main tool for shoppers to see how the animal
behind the meat was kept. Two postponements in seven months mean the transparency promise
made in 2023 is, three years on, still not enforceable.

Source: https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw03-de-tierhaltungskennzeichnungsgesetz-1134328
Bill text: https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/032/2103292.pdf

## 2. Reform of the label is coming — draft would tighten rules

**What happened:** BMLEH lists a "Drittes Gesetz zur Änderung des
Tierhaltungskennzeichnungsgesetzes"; in April 2026 the federal government also adopted a
draft Fifth Act amending the Animal Welfare Act (Tierschutzgesetz).

**Counterweight:** The Albert Schweitzer Stiftung, an animal-welfare NGO, judges the
ministry's draft as bringing "clear improvements" over the existing law — an unusual point
of agreement between an NGO and the ministry, and a reminder that the delay (item 1) and
the reform (item 2) are being traded against each other politically.

**Why it matters:** Whether the reform actually raises the bar, or just re-opens a settled
law for the industry, will be decided in the parliamentary committee stage. Watch this.

Sources: https://www.bmleh.de/SharedDocs/Gesetzestexte/DE/21-WP/3-AETierHaltKennzG.html

## 3. The money: ~€6.3 bn EU farm subsidies per year to Germany

**How much:** Around **€6.3 billion per year** in EU funds is available for agricultural
support in Germany over the 2023–2027 CAP period (BMLEH). BUND puts the same figure at
€6.3 bn/year for 2021–2027, with direct payments the largest share. EU-wide, 2023 saw
€38.16 bn in direct payments to farmers plus €12.95 bn for rural development.
Direct payments in Germany run at roughly **€280 per hectare per year**.

**The structural point:** Direct payments are paid per *hectare*, not per animal and not per
welfare outcome — so livestock operations benefit largely via their feed and forage area.
That is why campaigners argue the CAP money is effectively neutral-to-supportive of
intensive livestock, while farm groups argue it is basic income support keeping food prices
down and family farms alive.

**Why it matters:** This is roughly €75 per German resident per year of EU money flowing
into agriculture, with limited conditionality on animal welfare.

## 4. Lobbying: who pays for influence

**Germany — Deutscher Bauernverband (DBV):** In its Lobbyregister entry the DBV declares
lobbying expenditure of **€4,930,001 – €4,940,000 for 2024**, with roughly 20 full-time-
equivalent lobbyists (Lobbypedia reading of the register, status 27 Aug 2025). That makes it
one of the best-funded sectoral lobbies in Berlin.

**Brussels — Copa-Cogeca:** The EU farm-lobby umbrella declared roughly **€1.6 million** in
lobbying spend against **530 meetings** with EU officials; COGECA alone declared
€700,000–€799,999 in 2021. Total Brussels lobbying across all sectors is estimated at
**≥€1.5 bn/year**, up ~33 % since 2020.

**Balance:** Declared spend is not itself wrongdoing — farm associations are legitimate
interest representatives for hundreds of thousands of family businesses. The issue for
citizens is asymmetry: consumer, environmental and animal-welfare NGOs operate on a small
fraction of these budgets and hold far fewer meetings.

## 5. Science check: emissions down, antibiotics back up

**Emissions:** The Umweltbundesamt reports German agriculture's greenhouse-gas emissions
fell from **85.8 Mt CO₂-eq (1990) to 60.8 Mt CO₂-eq (2025), −29 %**. The Thünen-Institut's
inventory figure for 2024 is **53.3 Mt CO₂-eq** (different system boundary — the UBA number
includes energy-related and other agricultural sources). Most of the decline came from the
post-1990 collapse of the East German herd, not from recent policy.

**Antibiotics:** BVL data show antibiotic volumes handed to veterinarians fell for years —
539.9 t (2022), 529 t (2023, −11 t / −2.1 %) — but **rose again to about 562 t in 2024**.
Antibiotic use in livestock is a recognised driver of antimicrobial resistance in humans.

**Why it matters:** The industry's "we are getting greener" claim is supported for CO₂-eq
per the long trend, but the antibiotic reversal in 2024 cuts the other way and deserves a
parliamentary answer.
